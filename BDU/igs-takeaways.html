IGS Takeaways

Gates Competition Passing

Two evenly matched players pair up. One player dribbles through as many gates as possible in 1 minute while the other counts. Rest for 1 minute, then switch — the counter now dribbles while the original dribbler counts. The player with the lower total completes a brief exercise (e.g., jumping jacks) as a competitive consequence. Can also be run as a team competition.

Gates Competition Dribbling

Two evenly matched players pair up and pass to each other through as many gates as possible in 1 minute, tallying a combined score. Pairs compete against the rest of the group. Rest for 1 minute, then challenge another pair. The pair with the lower total completes a brief exercise (e.g., jumping jacks) as a competitive consequence. Also works great as a team competition and is an efficient way to collect cones at the end of practice.

Block Or One Time

Divide into three teams. One team starts at the goal post; the other two line up at the 18-yard box. The coach plays a ball in and the striking team attempts to score. The team on the post must block. If the strikers score, the defending team must touch the pole again before resuming. The striking team rotates to the back of the line. If the defense wins the ball, they move into the striking line — the losing strikers touch the pole and take on the defensive role. The coach does not play the next ball until the defending team has touched the pole.

Get Open

One player starts in the middle as a defender. Two players run back and forth, attempting to get open and complete a pass through the middle. A cone can be placed in between to create a designated passing lane.

Across The River

Two grids, two teams. For example, 5 vs. 2 — the larger team plays keep-away while the pair tries to win the ball and pass it to the opposite grid. If the larger team wins it back, they continue keep-away. If the smaller team is struggling, they can call for reinforcements. This drill develops movement, vision, timing, and ball control.

3v2 Transition

Set up three grids: the first runs a 3v2, the second is a transition zone, and the third hosts a 2v2. One team holds pinnies in hand. The 3v2 begins — if one of the two defenders wins the ball, they drop their pinnie and drive into the transition zone, looking to pass to their teammates in the next grid. They quickly become an active option as a new 3v2 begins under the same rules. If the ball goes out of bounds, one of the two wins possession, drops their pinnie, and advances to the transition zone to reset the drill. The dropped pinnie is picked up by a waiting player to form the next 2v2.

3 Team Keep Away

Set up four cone goals. Three teams wear different colors — for example, with 13 players: 5 blue, 5 yellow, and 3 red. Red plays for whichever team has possession. Teams score by completing a pass to a teammate through a gate. Set a target score (e.g., first to 5). Emphasize communication, controlled play, and movement.

Cone Passing

Cone spacing should vary by age and be spread across half the field. Three groups (red/blue/yellow) — distribute balls to approximately one-third of the players. Player A performs a skill, then passes to Player B following the color rotation (red → blue → yellow → red). Player A then moves to an open cone. Player B performs their skill and passes on. Sets last 2–3 minutes with 1 minute of rest between; run 5 sets. Space cones approximately 15 yards apart. Change the skill each set (10 quick touches, 3 rollovers, scissors, etc.) and vary the pass type each set (short, long, give-and-go, chip, etc.).

Long Passes 4 People

Groups of four — two players on each side facing each other. Focus on long passes: receive and trap, then play to the teammate beside you, who drives the ball across. Next, repeat the same trap-and-short-pass sequence but follow it with a lofting pass, cross, or chip. Finally, add a give-and-go or double-pass combination. Emphasize communication on both the receiving and sending end.

4 Person Movement Warmup

Four players move continuously for 1–2 minutes. Avoid circular patterns — players should move freely across the space. Progress through: two-touch, one-touch, one-bounce juggle, then no-bounce juggle. Can evolve to headers or combination sequences such as chest to thigh to foot. The goal is continuous movement and ball control.

Closing Fast Shot Fast

The goal of this drill is to practice closing down on defense while training the offense to shoot quickly. Place two goals approximately 20–25 yards apart and split the team. Each goal has a goalkeeper. Defenders must close quickly to prevent shots — never leave an attacker open. The offensive team should shoot quickly and frequently. Players should use communication and movement to create and deny opportunities. Excellent on-your-toes goalkeeper training as well.

Movement Skill Reset Find Target

Players run through the gate and call a teammate's name to receive a pass. They play the ball back while calling their own name, then run through the gate again to find another player to receive from. Run each round for 1–2 minutes, then rotate the middle player with a cone player. This format works across multiple skill sets: two-touch, volleys, headers, chest trap, thigh trap, foot trap, and turns.

4 Point Shooting

Set up four lines that rotate left to right. After each line shoots, they remain in play to follow up on the next line's shot. Line 1 receives a short pass, turns, and shoots quickly. Line 2 receives a pass from the coach for a one-touch shot, with a player from Line 1 in position for the rebound. Line 3 makes a move then shoots — players from Lines 1 and 2 are available for the rebound. Line 4 delivers a cross, with players from Lines 1, 2, and 3 in position for the finish. Repeat with the next group. Keep a good tempo throughout. Excellent goalkeeper training as well.

Practice Layout

Structured templates for a typical 3-hour practice and a lighter 2-hour day-after-game session.

    Typical Practice (~3 Hours)

    1. Warmup Running — Touchline to sideline to 10–15 yards out (e.g., dynamic runs along the lines)
    2. Warmup Passing Drill — Foot skills, 4-person movement and touches
    3. Stretch
    4. Skill Drill — 1v1 or 2v2 long touches: two-touch driven passes, chips, or 2-person give-and-gos
    5. Fitness — Ladders
    6. Lesson Drill — Focused on session theme (e.g., 4-point shooting, or defensive closing / short-field shoot and close)
    7. Fitness — 1-mile run (5 laps around the field)
    8. Crossing Drill — Develops drops, communication, and crossing technique
    9. Stretch

    Day After Game (~2 Hours)

    1. Warmup Running
    2. Stretch
    3. Movement Passing Drill
    4. Ladders
    5. Long Passing Drill
    6. Small-Sided Scrimmage — High touches, recovery pace
    7. Stretch

Circle Passing Warmup

The coach starts with 5 balls, feeding each one to a player around the circle. Each player controls the ball and passes it in one direction; the next player controls and passes on, then sprints to the next cone. Use communication throughout and move quickly. Add additional balls as the rhythm develops. Focus on a sharp first touch followed by a crisp pass. Reverse direction after each round. If the flow breaks down, stop and reset.
